I study how measurement theory and psychometrics can guide the assessment of both human (e.g., psychological traits, knowledge and skills) and machine (e.g., AI model capability). My research identifies practical questions in these domains that cannot be adequately addressed with existing statistical tools, and I develop new statistical tools that better address them.

I received my Ph.D. in quantitative psychology from UIUC in 2018 and was a postdoctoral research scientist in the Department of Statistics at Columbia University from 2018 to 2020.

Current research interests

  • Developing measurement theory and psychometric tools for unstructured test response data (e.g., process data, constructed responses)
  • Adapting LLMs to support development of measurement theory-grounded, evidence-centered assessments for learning (e.g., diagnostic assessments, simulation-based tasks)
  • Measurement theory and new psychometric tools for AI model evaluation and benchmark design

STAT 428: Statistical Computing

Instructor: Susu Zhang (szhan105 at illinois dot edu)

Canvas Syllabus (1/16/2024)

Course description

Statistical computing uses computer-intensive methods to address statistical problems. These methods support statistical inference and parameter estimation, both frequentist and Bayesian. The course explains why and how the algorithms work, with examples, and how to implement them in R.

  • Random variable generation
  • Monte Carlo integration and inference
  • Resampling methods (bootstrap, permutation tests)
  • Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)
  • Numerical analysis tools (nonlinear equation solving, optimization, numerical integration)
  • The expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm

PSYC 490: Measurement and Test Development Lab

Instructor: Susu Zhang (szhan105 at illinois dot edu)
Teaching assistant: Sunbeom Kwon

Learn@Illinois Syllabus (8/21/2023)

Course description

Measurement assigns numbers to individuals to represent cognitive and noncognitive properties, such as abilities, progress, attitudes and personality. Developing reliable and valid instruments is central to measurement theory, which overlaps with psychometrics. Measurement places more weight on concepts and tasks, and psychometrics on statistical methods.

Covered topics

  • Fundamental concepts: reliability and validity
  • Psychometric theories: classical test theory and item response theory
  • Test construction and evaluation: item analysis, equating and scaling
  • Practical topics: adaptive testing, cognitive diagnosis, detection of noneffortful responses and automated scoring
